# Mentl through the lens of the vision — the whole tree, and three designs (2026-09-25)

**What this is.** The audit (`docs/PROCESS-AUDIT-2026-09-25.md`) measured the
process. This document does the other thing Morgan asked for: it looks at
every part of the tree through the vision the early documents state (the
medium proposes, proves, teaches, explores in parallel, and raises the person
at the keyboard), and then it takes the three questions the vision cannot
leave open and designs answers rather than reporting that nobody has them.
Every claim about the artifact was read from the artifact this session; every
design is marked as a design, with the invariant it keeps, the gate that
would see it red, and the proof it still owes. The negation design was
handed to an adversarial pass before this was committed; its findings are in
§7.

**Papert's promise, since the audit used the phrase.** Seymour Papert's
*Mindstorms* (1980) argued that a computer can be an object to think with: a
medium in which powerful ideas become things a learner builds, runs, breaks
and fixes, so that the ideas are learned the way a child in France learns
French, by living in them. Logo's turtle was the first such medium; the
promise was that learning to program in a well-made medium teaches thinking
itself, that a bug is information rather than failure, and that the learner
teaches the computer and is taught by the attempt. The promise was only
partly kept: the Logo studies found modest transfer, and the environments
that followed (Scratch, the constructionist line) kept the feedback loop but
not the powerful ideas. Mentl's claim that picking up the language teaches
programming is that promise with proof as the feedback: the gradient is the
turtle's immediate response, the Why chain is the bug as information, and a
refusal that names the claim it could not discharge is the medium teaching
the learner back. Nobody has measured an environment on that promise; §4.4
of the audit lists the measurement.

**The finding the map makes.** The medium is about ten percent medium. The
mass is a compiler being a compiler (substrate, judgment, codegen, spine:
roughly 45,000 lines) and a process being a process (8,300 lines of gates,
300,000 words of prose). The parts that are the vision's differentiators, the
proposer, the gradient, the cursor and the voice, total about 5,000 lines and
have no test of their quality. That is not a criticism of the compiler work;
a medium that proposes needs a judgment that is right, and the judgment is
right. It is the direction of marginal investment: every next hour that goes
into restructuring the judgment for its own sake is an hour not spent on the
five thousand lines where the vision lives, and the five thousand lines have
nothing to be measured on because there is no program to propose into.

**Specific findings, by part.**

- `synth_proposer`: the proof gate is real (`candidate_proven` unifies the
  candidate's cell with the hole's cell and discharges the refinement inside
  a checkpoint, then rolls back). The candidate space is shallow: vocabulary
  calls are zero-argument only, so a hole of function type in a pipeline can
  be filled by `f` but never by `f(cfg)` or `x |> f`; there is no composition
  and no bounded sub-hole recursion, so the medium cannot propose a
  two-step move. Ranking is proximity alone. The divergence classifier is
  the most original piece in the file and it reads live.
- `cursor`: the argmax is built, holes outrank annotations, proximity decays
  by scope. The "cursor moves itself" demo needs only a transport that
  re-renders on graph delta; the substrate is there.
- `mentl`: the gradient proposes annotations from a fixed lattice (Pure,
  !Alloc, !IO, !Network, own, ref, refinements) by speculative apply, verify
  and rollback. Right mechanism, small lattice, no capability the developer
  did not already know about; the proactive gradient the field lacks is here
  in seed form.
- `egraph`: six rules, directed to cheaper forms, effect-gated. The comment
  calls it equality saturation; it is a simplifier whose union-find has one
  member per class (§3).
- `effects` + the gate in `infer`: the crown is careful and its instance law
  is genuinely beyond Flix (bare versus instance, provable distinctness). Its
  open class is one shape, measured: a declared negation on a function whose
  body row is a parameter's free row variable is checked against nothing
  (`fn run(f) with !E = f()` accepts `run(() => op())`). §2 designs the fix.
- `verify`: an interval fragment and a ledger with honest debt, no SMT. The
  field is well ahead here and the design already says so.
- `lower`/`backends`: the twin emission, the repr gradient, the fold leaves
  and the structural equality are the form half done as handlers, which is
  the vision's own answer to "the compiler chooses the form" (§3).
- `lib/persist`, `lib/threading`, `lib/search`: persist is one memory copy,
  threads spawn over the shared image, search is the Choice effect with
  backtracking handlers. The TIME and SPACE substrates exist as library, and
  nothing composes them into the fused search yet.
- `ide/`: the boot in the browser is a real achievement and the page's five
  surfaces are the right ones; the one thing to hold it to is the design
  system's own rule that the projection never takes the keyboard away.
- `tests/`: 480 fixtures prove the compiler. The medium's proposals have zero
  fixtures. A proposal fixture is a hole plus the expected verdict (fill,
  ask with a named divergence, or none); the frontier harness could hold
  them today.

## 2 · Design: negation sound under polymorphism and across worlds

### 2.1 What the artifact does today, read at the definitions

A row is `EfRow(present, absent, tail)` read as `present ∪ (tail ∖ absent)`,
with the tail `EtClosed`, `EtOpen(edges)` (a set of row-cell handles whose
live content joins in) or `EtAll` (everything except the absent set). Two
different things currently live in the `absent` field:

- **A mask.** `~> h` subtracts `handled(h)`: `diff_row` mints a fresh cell
  bound to `([], mask, EtOpen(edges))` so the subtraction rides every union
  as one opaque edge. This is a *filter on a reading* and it is correct.
- **A prohibition.** A declared `!E` becomes the gate row `([], [E], EtAll)`
  and is checked by `row_subsumes(body, gate)` at declaration exit. When the
  body's tail is open, the declared absences are also written into the
  cell's absent field "so `!E` constrains every instantiation". This is a
  *constraint on a variable*, and it is stored in the same field as the mask.

The gate fires once, at declaration exit, against whatever the body row is at
that moment. When the body row chases to a free cell (the effect-polymorphic
HOF: `fn run(f) with !E = f()` has `row(run) = row(f)`, a parameter's own
free cell), the gate's `NRowFree` arm does nothing, and nothing is written
that a later instantiation could refuse. Measured in RESIDUE on 2026-08-18:
`run(() => op())` compiles with zero diagnostics, and so do the `Pure` and
the unrelated `!WASI` forms. The residue's own conclusion is right: "the
constraint belongs at the DECLARATION, where inference owns the write", and
rejecting free tails at the *read* is the twice-measured dead end (646 false
mismatches on the self-compile). Two more facts shape the design: the ETALL
law (a stored `EtAll` in an inferred row fabricates "everything except", so
`EtAll` may exist only in a gate value, never in a cell), and the population
of the fix (eleven declared-row HOFs across the eight largest modules,
counted by the medium's own `census declared-row-hof`).

### 2.2 The design: the gate is carried by the cell

**One representation change.** A free row cell carries an optional gate:

```
NRowFree(gate: Option(EffRow))     // today: NRowFree(label)
```

The gate is a *declared* row in the form `row_subsumes` already accepts as
its second argument: a closed positive row (`with A + B`), a negation row
(`([], [E, F], EtAll)`), or their meet. Nothing else changes in `EffRow`;
`EtAll` never enters a cell, because the gate is a constraint *on* the cell,
not a value *in* it. The absent field keeps exactly one job: the mask.

**Four rules, each at a write inference already owns.**

1. **Declaration.** At `enforce_row_gate`, the `NRowFree` arm stops doing
   nothing. If the body row chases to a free cell, the declared row is
   installed as that cell's gate (meet with any gate already there). A
   declared `Pure` is the closed empty row: the cell is bound to it
   outright, because "at most nothing" has exactly one solution. The
   `NRowBound` arm is unchanged: a determined body is checked as today.
2. **Bind.** Every bind of a free cell to a row (the `bind_edges_to` sites in
   `unify_row_canonical`, the frame's exit bind, the join's bind) first
   checks `row_subsumes(row, gate)`; a failure is `E_EffectMismatch` at the
   site that tried the bind, carrying both spans. If the bound row's tail is
   `EtOpen(edges)`, the gate is pushed onto every edge cell (meet with each
   edge's own gate), because the cell's reading will include those edges'
   content. If an edge is a mask cell (`([], mask, EtOpen(inner))`), the gate
   pushed past it is the gate with the mask's names removed from its absents
   and added to its presents' admission: absorption satisfies negation
   structurally, which is what `sound-masking-negation` pins.
3. **Var meets var.** In the `EtOpen ~ EtOpen` arm, the edges private to one
   side bind to a residual whose tail is the other side's edge set; the
   binding side's gates are pushed onto that set. Gates meet as rows meet:
   presents intersect where both are closed, absents union, and a closed
   gate meeting a negation gate keeps the closed presents and the union of
   absents.
4. **Generalize and instantiate.** A quantified row variable keeps its gate
   in the scheme (this is the qualified-type discipline: `∀r. (E ∉ r) ⇒ …`).
   `instantiate` mints the fresh row cell *with the same gate*
   (`graph_fresh_row` gains the gate argument; `quantify_ctor_row` and the
   `subst_ty` row mint are the two sites). A forward reference through a
   pre-registered signature instantiates a gated var exactly like a
   finished one, which closes the shape `forward-hof-row-underpublish`
   belonged to.

**What the reads keep.** `row_subsumes` stays a read with no binds, exactly
as its contract says. The projections that call it (`is_pure`, the census,
the gradient) see gates only through the bind sites that already refused,
so no projection becomes a writer.

**What it dissolves.**

- The vacuous class: `fn run(f) with !E = f()` gates `f`'s cell with `!E`;
  `run(() => op())` binds `[E]` into a gated cell at the call site and is
  refused *there*, with the declaration's span and the call's span in one
  diagnostic. A callback within the cap is admitted untouched, so the
  false-mismatch dead end is not revisited: a gate bites only on a violation.
- The ETALL law's hazard: no negation is ever stored as a row value, so
  nothing can fabricate "everything except" into an inferred row.
- The two homes for `absent`: the field is the mask; the gate is the
  constraint. `absent_minus_present`, the contradiction report, and the join
  law lose their double reading.
- The `Pure` case becomes monomorphic where it must be and nowhere else.

**The invariant.** *Every cell whose content can flow into a gated cell's
reading is gated at least as strongly, modulo the masks between them, and no
gated cell is ever bound to a row that fails its gate.* Rules 2 and 3 are the
propagation half; rule 4 is the persistence half across generalization.

### 2.3 The theorem, and the proof it owes

**Statement.** Let `f` declare row `D`. If the program is accepted, then in
every execution, every operation performed during the evaluation of `f`'s
body that is not absorbed by a handler installed within that evaluation has a
name that `D` admits: present in `D`'s positive set when `D` is closed, and
not forbidden by `D`'s absent set under the instance law (`eff_forbids`).

**Proof plan.** The calculus is the row-and-handler calculus of Biernacki,
Piróg, Polesiuk and Sieczkowski (POPL 2019, dynamic innermost dispatch) with
Rémy-style absence constraints on row variables; Flix's ICFP 2023 effect
safety theorem is the instance without handlers. The dynamic row of an
evaluation is the multiset of unabsorbed operations. Preservation follows
from four lemmas: (i) *gate preservation*: unify, join and the frame bind
never bind a gated cell to a row failing its gate, and push gates as rule 2
says; (ii) *generalization*: a scheme's quantified row var carries its gate
and every instance is minted with it; (iii) *install soundness*: the mask
minted at `~>` corresponds to the handler's absorbed set under innermost
dispatch, and the handler's own arms are judged inside the install's scope
so their row joins the result (the 2026-08-18 state-init fix is this
lemma's corner); (iv) *resume charges the residual* (§2.4). Progress is
unchanged. Lemma (i) is the new work; (ii) is standard; (iii) is what the
crown battery already pins as crucibles; (iv) is §2.4.

### 2.4 Across worlds: the continuation's row is its residual, charged at resume

`TCont(R, S, discipline, world)` already carries a row. The design fixes its
meaning: **the world of a continuation is the residual row of its remainder,
the effects the remainder may perform when resumed.** Then:

- `resume(k, v)` is typed `R -> S with world(k)`: resuming performs the
  remainder, exactly as calling a closure performs its latent row. A resume
  site inside a `with !E` refuses when `E ∈ world(k)`. For a one-shot
  handler arm the remainder is the install's own body, whose row the tee
  already accounts for, so nothing changes on the stack-only path; the
  world is load-bearing for reified and persisted continuations, which is
  where it was inert.
- A persisted image stores the residual row as data beside the record.
  `mentl resume <image>` compares it against the resuming site's declared
  row at load with the same `row_subsumes` read, and refuses with a located
  `E_ResumeWorldMismatch` that names the captured world and the resuming
  gate. That is a typed refusal at a deserialization boundary, which is the
  honest form: an image is data, so the check is at load, and it is at
  compile time exactly when the image's type is statically known (an image
  produced and consumed by one program).

### 2.5 Install identity, when the program needs it: the world as an instance

Mentl dispatches an operation to the innermost installed handler, and the
crown's crucibles pin that (`sound-escape-birth-evidence`). That is sound for
negation, because the mask is applied at evaluation; what it leaves open is
*which* install absorbs when two handlers for the same effect are live, the
accidental-capture question capability calculi close by lexical binding
(Zhang and Myers's tunneling; Effekt; the capture calculus). The medium's own
vocabulary already has the mechanism: a parameterized effect `Sample(44100)`
is an instance whose argument is compared by provable distinctness. So the
design is: **a handler value may be an instance argument.** `op` performed
under `E(h)` targets install `h`; the row carries `E(h)`; `!E(h)` forbids that
install and no other; two handler values with distinct handles are provably
distinct, which the existing `eff_args_provably_distinct` extends with one
arm. Dynamic innermost dispatch stays the default; lexical capture is what a
program writes when it names the install. This is the "modal world-index"
stated in the medium's own terms, and it needs handler values to be typed,
which `Hβ.syntax.handler-chain-is-a-value` already owes for other reasons.

### 2.7 Implementation sites and cost

`types.mn`: the `NRowFree` payload. `effects.mn`: `unify_row_canonical`'s
three `bind_edges_to` calls gain the check and the push; `row_join` and the
frame exit bind gain the check; `diff_row`'s mask cell gains the push-through
rule; a `gate_meet`. `infer.mn`: `enforce_row_gate`'s `NRowFree` arm installs
the gate; `graph_fresh_row` takes a gate; `instantiate` and
`quantify_ctor_row` copy it. `pipeline.mn`: the root gate reads the install

battery plus the new fixtures; the eleven wheel sites are the first
population. The risk that the adversarial pass was pointed at: propagation
through multi-edge tails and through masks, and the meet of two gates.

## 3 · Design: is the e-graph the most powerful thing for the form half?

### 3.1 What is built

`egraph.mn` is 318 lines. Equivalence is one more graph edge (`graph_canon_set`,
chased by `graph_canon_at`), every rule reads its operands through the chase
so congruence closes across passes, and a dropping rewrite fires only when the
dropped operand's row is provably pure. Six rules: the additive and
multiplicative identities, absorption by zero, subtraction and division
identities, and constant folding. Every rule points a node at a strictly
cheaper node, so each class has one member and "extraction" is the chase.
`saturate_range` runs the same rules over the fan's freshly minted candidate
space so survivors that are the same form collapse before a tie is asked
about. That is a directed simplifier with an effect gate and a good home,
not equality saturation: nothing keeps two forms of one class alive to be
chosen between later, which is the entire reason equality saturation exists
(the phase-ordering problem of non-monotone rewrites such as reassociation,
distribution and fusion).

### 3.2 The honest answer

Three things in the design are right and should be kept exactly.

- **The fork/merge duality.** Meaning is explored by forking (candidates
  conflict, so each verifies in a rolled-back world); form is explored by
  merging (members are equal by construction, so nothing is undone). A tie
  in form is free and a tie in meaning is a question. This is the principle
  that makes the oracle and the optimizer one substrate, and it is not in
  the literature in that form.
- **Effect-gated rewriting.** egg rewrites syntactically; the field handles
  effects by relaxing a control-flow skeleton. Reading each operand's row and
  refusing a dropping rewrite unless the row subsumes pure is cleaner and,
  per the fourth research pass, untested by anyone.
- **Equivalence with a Reason.** Every canon edge is a graph edge and can
  carry why it was drawn. No production optimizer can answer "why was my code
  compiled this way" with a walkable chain. That is human-empowering in the
  vision's own sense and it costs nothing extra.

Two things should not be done next.

- **A whole-program saturating optimizer.** Extraction with shared subterms
  is NP-hard and hard to approximate (OOPSLA 2024); saturation blows up;
  static costs mislead on real hardware, which is why AlphaDev and STOKE
  measure. Building that engine competes for the same hours as the
  proposer, the gradient and the cursor, and its payoff is codegen quality
  that no program yet asks for.
- **Treating the e-graph as the vision's "compiler chooses the form".** The
  form choices the developer can see and steer are already handlers: the
  schedule (`Seq | Thread | Simd | Gpu`) read live at the install, the repr
  gradient pinning widths, the twin emission. Those are the human-empowering
  half, because the gradient can name them and the developer can accept them.
  Saturation is opaque by nature.

### 3.3 The ultimate form, when a program asks for it

- **One union-find for types and terms.** The graph already holds an HM
  union-find over type cells with a Reason on every merge. Term cells are the
  same mechanism one kind over: a class is a cell, a member is an edge, a
  merge is a bind with a Reason, and extraction is a read. There is no e-graph
  data structure because there is no separate type union-find either; the
  `egraph.mn` comment already says this, and the design is to mean it: keep
  members when a rule is non-monotone, keep the directed chase when it is.
- **Cost is a handler.** Extraction's cost function is `~> Cost`: static size
  for the fan's dedup, a cycle model for the emit, and *measured latency*
  through the runner for the audio path. Superoptimization with a measured
  cost is what the field found necessary, and in Mentl it is a handler swap
  the developer can see in `where`'s badge.
- **Tractable extraction from the surface, stated as a conjecture to prove.**
  Programs written in the five verbs are series-parallel graphs with a
  bounded number of feedback edges; series-parallel graphs have treewidth
  at most two, and each `<~` adds at most one. The OOPSLA 2024 extraction
  algorithm is polynomial and optimal for bounded treewidth. If the rewrite
  set is restricted to rules that preserve verb structure (fusion,
  reassociation within a chain, identity, folding), the e-graph of a Mentl
  program keeps bounded treewidth and optimal extraction is polynomial. That
  would be a property of the surface no arbitrary IR can offer, and it is
  the precise form of "extraction gets smaller, not cleverer". It is a
  conjecture; §7 records the adversarial pass's verdict on it, and the proof
  obligation is to show the rule set preserves the bound, since `<|` fanout
  creates sharing.

**Verdict.** The e-graph is the right principle and the wrong next build. Keep
the three things above, grow the rule set only when Pulse's audio path asks
(fusion of adjacent stages is the first rule a renderer will want), make cost
a handler before adding rules, and treat the treewidth conjecture as the
research note that turns a heuristic into a guarantee.

## 4 · Design: the ranker, and what "the medium proposes" can honestly mean

The measurements (audit §4.4) are unambiguous: where proof search and learned
ranking have been compared, learned ranking wins by wide margins, and every
best result is a hybrid in which proof filters and a model ranks. The vision
does not need to lose to this; it needs to say what is uniquely its own.

- **The filter is the medium's and it is total.** Nothing surfaces that did
  not survive inference and proof in a rolled-back world. A ranker, whatever
  it is, chooses among proven things. That sentence is already true of the
  built proposer and it is stronger than any claim about models.
- **Constraints compound; guesses do not.** Every acceptance and every
  rejection at a hole lands as a Reason edge on the graph (the accepted
  candidate's Reason, the divergence answered). Those are constraints on the
  next proposal at the same and neighbouring positions: an answered
  divergence narrows the space forever for this project. A model's ranking
  never becomes a constraint. Over a project's life the medium's proposer
  converges toward the project's intent while a model's accuracy plateaus at
  the corpus. This is the "gradient learns THIS project" claim of
  ULTIMATE_MEDIUM §8.4, stated as a mechanism: the ranker's memory is the
  graph.
- **The space is small exactly when the constraints are, and that is a
  measurement, not a slogan.** Measured this session on the built fan: a
  hole of type `Positive` (a refinement) yields one survivor and fills; a
  bare `Int` hole in a scope with twenty nullary integer functions yields 23
  survivors and asks a question that can only say "nothing here bounds the
  value"; a function-typed hole in a pipe stage (`5 |> ??` with three
  unary functions in scope) yields *no proposal at all*, because the
  vocabulary enumerator proposes zero-argument calls and never a function
  reference or a stage. So the honest shape of the claim is: at next-move
  scope the medium wins when the position carries a real constraint, asks
  usefully only when the divergence has content, and today cannot see the
  most common shape in a Mentl program, the pipe stage. Lean's `exact?` is
  the evidence that enumeration plus proof beats generation on constrained
  goals; the gradient's job is to make positions constrained (that is what
  "annotations are inputs" means), and the proposer's first growth is the
  stage.
- **Ranking is a handler, and the medium's own ranker is symbolic and
  explainable.** `rank_of` reads proximity today. The design adds two
  signals no model has because they are project-local provenance: *Reason
  overlap* (how much of a candidate's Reason chain shares ancestry with the
  constraints stated near the hole, so "proposed because it carries the
  `Sample` refinement you declared at line 12" is a true sentence) and
  *acceptance history* (what this developer took and refused at this shape
  before). Cost from `~> Cost` breaks form ties. Every signal renders as a
  sentence at the address surface.
- **A model plugs in behind the gate and is measured on the same fixture.**
  The Synth chain already admits `synth_llm` as a peer handler. The design
  adds the honest consequence: the medium keeps the gradient log (proposal,
  verdict, what was written) as a fixture, and any ranker, symbolic or
  learned, local or remote, is scored on it. A tiny local ranker trained on
  the project's own accepted moves is the vision-consistent form of a
  learned ranker: private, offline, grown from constraints.

**The claim, stated so it survives the evidence.** The medium owns the
candidate space and the verdict; proof is the filter; ranking is a handler
whose default is symbolic and explainable and whose memory is the graph; a tie
the ranker cannot break is a question, and the answer compounds. "No model is
advantageous at any scope" is retired until the next-move benchmark on Pulse
says otherwise; "the model is unemployed" was never the claim worth making.

## 7 · The adversarial pass

Two Fable passes were dispatched against §2 and against §3–§4, with
instructions to refute by constructing programs and running them. The first
dispatch was killed by the session's rate limit before either read a file;
both were relaunched when usage returned. Their reports are folded in below
as they arrive. The measurements that did not need a fresh mind were taken
inline first.

### 7.1 The negation class, measured on the boot (`mentl check`, then `mentl run`)

| Shape | Today |
|---|---|
| `fn run(f) with !E = f()` · `run(() => op())` under an outer handler for `E` | accepts, and **runs, exit 7**: `E` is performed inside a function declared `!E` and absorbed outside it |
| the same with `with Pure` | accepts |
| `fn run(f) with A = f()` · callback performs `B` (positive bound) | accepts |
| `fn both(f, g) with !E = f() + g()` · one callback performs `E` | accepts |
| `fn ap(f, x) with !E = f(x)` · `ap((y) => op() + y, 1)` | accepts |
| `fn run(f) with A + !E = f()` · callback performs `E` | accepts |
| `main` defined before `run` (forward reference) | accepts |
| `fn run(f) with !E = (f()) ~> h`, `h` absorbs `E` | accepts (correct: the mask satisfies the gate) |
| `fn run(f) with !E = f()` · callback performs `F` | accepts (correct: within the cap) |
| `fn mk() with !E = () => op()` · `fn outer() with !E = mk()()` | refuses at `outer` (correct: the escaped call is charged) |

Every row whose body row is a parameter's free cell leaks, in both the
negative and the positive direction, and the leak reaches the executable.
The mask and the escaped-call cases are right, which is what lets the design
in §2 keep `row_subsumes` and `diff_row` unchanged and put the whole fix at
the bind.
